## The question

Following a Hawai'i climate-related disruption such as the 2023 Maui Wildfires, did households below the poverty line experience greater difficulty accessing medical care and medications than households above the poverty line?

## Why it matters

This matters because disaster recovery is not only about rebuilding infrastructure, but also about protecting households and workers from preventable economic and health harms.  Communities are affected especially lower-income households, rural and neighbor-island residents, people with chronic health needs, children, and elderly population.  Higher prices can lead to food insecurity, delayed medical treatment, and reduced ability to manage chronic illness.  Workers involved in recover or essential services may face unstable schedules and pressure to work even when conditions are unsafe.  From a public health nursing perspective, it matters because nurses see the consequences of food insecurity, transportation barriers, interrupted care, medication problems, and unequal recovery.  From an occupational health nursing perspective, it matters because essential workers may be expected to work unsafe work conditions and deal with higher household expenses. 

## Hypothesis

I expect to find Hawai'i lower-income households will experience a greater burden because necessities have relatively inelastic demand and constitute a larger share of lower-income household budgets.  Following the August 8, 2023 Maui wildfires, households below the poverty line experienced greater barriers to medical care and medication access than households above the poverty line because essential expenses consume a larger share of limited household resources, reducing their ability to absorb disaster-related income and financial shocks.

## What would falsify it

The hypothesis would not be supported if MauiWES data show no meaningful difference in post-wildfire medical-care and medication-access difficulties between households below and above the poverty line.
